    Doctoral Coursework in Philosophy (2023–2025)¶

    From September 2023 to June 2025 I studied at the Doctoral School of Philosophy of the University of Pécs (head of school: Prof. János Boros). As a doctoral student without an MA in philosophy, I took the school's compulsory course Philosophy I / History of Philosophy in all four semesters and passed the complex examination in philosophy in June 2025.

    Since November 2026 I continue my doctorate at the University of Pécs Doctoral School of Literary and Cultural Studies, after the Doctoral School of Philosophy closed. This page records the syllabi of the course as published in the school's official semester programmes.

    At a glance
    Course Philosophy I / History of Philosophy
    Period Semesters 1–4, September 2023 – June 2025
    Format Weekly online seminar (MS Teams) with an English summary, in-person study days in Pécs, research seminars
    Assessment Attendance and one essay of 3–5 pages per semester
    Credits 30 per semester; 120 by the end of semester 4
    Final examination Complex examination in philosophy, June 2025

    How the course worked¶

    Each semester had the same structure:

    • Online seminar. A weekly session on Tuesday afternoon, given by a different lecturer of the school each week. Sessions ended with a summary in English.
    • Study days. Two or three compulsory in-person days in Pécs, with lectures on topics outside the weekly seminar.
    • Research seminars and consultations. Students chose at least one research seminar or individual consultation with a teacher of the school.
    • Semester essay. One essay of 3–5 pages on the topic that interested the student most, submitted to the lecturer of that topic, who confirmed completion to the doctoral school.

    The shared reading for every session was the relevant entry in the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy and the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, with further literature recommended by each lecturer. According to the programmes, the course provided the knowledge of the history of philosophy required for the complex examination.

    Semester Theme My semester essay
    Autumn 2023 History of philosophy: Enlightenment to the Vienna Circle “Criticism of Parmenides by Plato” (for Dóra Rippl)
    Spring 2024 Twentieth-century and contemporary philosophy “On Modal Metaphysics” (for László Kocsis)
    Autumn 2024 Ancient philosophy, logic, epistemology, metaphysics “The Struggle for Rationality” (for László Halász, submitted March 2025)¹
    Spring 2025 Survey from antiquity to modern times; applied philosophy “From Counting Angels to Counting Likes” (for László Halász, philosophy of science; revised version resubmitted May 2025)¹

    Essay titles link to the versions published on Academia.edu, where some essays appear under revised titles. ¹ “The Struggle for Rationality” was later retitled From Counting Angels to Counting Likes: Science, Faith, and the Rise of Post-Truth; László Halász reviewed it under the new title, and the same text served as the fourth-semester essay.

    Complex examination in philosophy · June 2025¶

    The complex examination closes the first, coursework-based phase of a Hungarian doctorate. I passed it at the end of my fourth semester, in June 2025.

    Admission requirements

    • 120 credits by the end of the fourth semester, with all grades recorded
    • A research bibliography of at least 30 items, including international literature
    • A research plan of up to two pages

    Format. A public, oral examination before a committee of at least three members, all holding doctoral degrees, at least one third of them from outside the university. The supervisor is not a member of the committee but may give a written opinion.

    Structure. Two parts, each with two topics:

    • Theoretical part

      1. History of philosophy, from the Presocratics to the present: the candidate presents their own view of its history and discusses selected periods or philosophers in detail.
      2. Systematic philosophy: one branch (for example epistemology, ethics or philosophy of science) or one author, preferably connected to the dissertation, with its current literature.

    • Dissertation part

      1. The dissertation topic: why it was chosen, and a review of the current literature on it.
      2. Work so far: results achieved and what the dissertation adds to existing research.

    Source: official semester programmes of the Doctoral School of Philosophy, University of Pécs (autumn 2023 – spring 2025), and the school’s notice on the complex examination (May 2025).
